# FAQ (plain language, not part of the licence)

**Can I use Ultrastable for free?** Yes, for non-commercial use: personal projects, learning, teaching, published research, non-profits, schools, public bodies, and a 60-day no-revenue evaluation by anyone.

**I run a startup. Do I owe anything?** If Ultrastable runs, builds or supports a business that earns money, yes: register under the Commercial Terms. The default Founder tier is free until the business has earned USD 100,000 in total, then 5% (2% on the part of a year's revenue above USD 1,000,000).

**I am a consultant and a client pays me.** That is Commercial Use (`LICENSE` 3.1(d), 3.2). Register.

**I use it at my employer, internally.** If the employer is for-profit, that is Commercial Use after the 60-day evaluation. See the Internal tier.

**I use it at a university.** Non-commercial if the work is published or public. If you start a spin-out that earns money, the spin-out needs a licence.

**Can I host it as a service?** Not without a separate agreement. A hosted service is the case the licence reserves most tightly (`LICENSE` 3.1(b), 4.1(c)).

**Can I fork it?** Yes, non-commercially. A fork must keep this licence, publish its source, keep the notices and offer source to its users if you run it over a network (`LICENSE` Section 6).

**Can I write my own implementation from the Specification?** Non-commercially, yes. Commercially it needs a licence if you used the Specification to build it (`LICENSE` 7.2). If you wrote it without ever seeing the Specification, the licence does not bind you, and you are not infringing the Licensor's copyright. Protection comes from copyright, trademark, contract and the commercial price list.

**What does the licence say about patents?** `LICENSE` Section 10 gives users a non-commercial patent license, ends a user's licences if they bring a patent claim against the Materials, and requires anyone who files a patent application that touches the Materials to cite the published Specification as prior art. The Specification is published on purpose and dated.

**Is it open source?** No. It restricts commercial use, which the Open Source Definition does not allow. It is source-available.

**Can an AI company train on it?** Not for a commercial model without a written agreement (`LICENSE` Section 9). Running AI agents to operate the Software is fine.

**What does a breach cost?** For unlicensed Commercial Use: three times the Commercial Terms for the period (or a reasonable royalty, if more), plus enforcement costs, plus an injunction (`LICENSE` Section 5). Stopping and disclosing promptly may earn reinstatement at the normal price.

**What if a country does not enforce this?** The licence picks Swiss law and ICC arbitration, which is enforceable through the New York Convention in about 170 countries, and it lets the Licensor go to any local court for an injunction. Enforcement against a small user abroad is a cost-benefit decision for the Licensor, not a guarantee.
