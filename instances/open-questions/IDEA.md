# Idea

What science honestly does not know yet: the big open questions, where the evidence leans today, and how that lean has moved. Knowing what is not yet known is as useful as knowing what is; a map of open questions, with where the evidence leans and how fast it moves, is missing from public knowledge. An atlas of open questions by field, each with the competing answers, the best evidence for each, a graded lean and a history of how the lean moved. Every entry follows the Unlearn shape: start from why the intuition feels right, ask the reader to guess, then show what the evidence found, graded honestly by study design.

# System test

- Loop: A weekly beat watches review and preprint feeds for each question, files new evidence on the entry and moves the lean only when graded evidence moves it, recording why.
- Measure: share of questions whose evidence feed was read within 7 days, target 1.0; lean changes logged with graded evidence
- Upkeep: new studies, data releases and evaluations arrive every week, sources rot and entries must be re-graded
- Run cost: 5 USD a month for the back end

# Hook

- Pick your lean on a live question, then see where the evidence leans and how it moved. Knowing you hold a minority view, or that nobody knows yet, is the hook.

# Public good

- Honest public knowledge of what is not known protects people from false certainty and points students and funders at questions worth answering.

# Why people return

- Readers record their own lean and see questions move toward an answer; a question that closes becomes an Unlearn or Practical Solutions entry.

# First outcomes

- Phase 1: atlas of 30 open questions with graded leans
- Phase 2: lean history and reader leans
- Phase 3: links into Unlearn and Practical Solutions when a question settles

# Constraints

- Budget per month: 5
- No accounts, cookies or trackers; reader taps are counted per entry with nothing that identifies the reader
- Every number on a page comes from a cited, graded source; nothing is typed without one
