# Idea

Before you decide, see what usually happens: graded base rates for life, work and policy choices, and your own forecasting record. People and institutions predict from the story in front of them and ignore how often such things usually go; showing the reference class first makes forecasts better. An encyclopedia of reference classes (new businesses surviving five years, megaprojects on budget, diets kept after a year, reforms delivering their stated aim), each with its base rate, source and grade. Every entry follows the Unlearn shape: start from why the intuition feels right, ask the reader to guess, then show what the evidence found, graded honestly by study design.

# System test

- Loop: An hourly beat re-reads each entry's source series for new releases and surfaces newer studies; readers' guesses before the reveal measure which base rates surprise most and order the site.
- Measure: share of entries whose source series was re-read within 7 days, target 1.0; share of readers whose guess missed the base rate by more than half, published per entry
- Upkeep: new studies, data releases and evaluations arrive every week, sources rot and entries must be re-graded
- Run cost: 5 USD a month for the back end

# Hook

- Guess first: how often do new restaurants survive five years? Then the base rate, and how far off you were. Every person misses some, and the miss is the hook.

# Public good

- People and governments make better decisions about money, health and policy when they see how such things usually go; it counters overconfident plans that waste public money.

# Why people return

- A reader keeps a private forecasting record in their own browser, sees how calibrated they are, and comes back when a base rate they followed moves.

# First outcomes

- Phase 1: encyclopedia of 50 graded base rates with guess-first entries
- Phase 2: personal calibration record and follow-ups
- Phase 3: decision companion: name a plan, get its reference class and a pre-mortem

# Constraints

- Budget per month: 5
- No accounts, cookies or trackers; reader taps are counted per entry with nothing that identifies the reader
- Every number on a page comes from a cited, graded source; nothing is typed without one
