# Idea

Every big policy promised something. See what it promised, what the evaluations found since, and how the verdict moved. Policies are judged by their announcements, not their results; holding each one to its own stated aim, as evaluations arrive, changes how people argue about the next one. A casebook of policies and programmes worldwide, each with its stated aim in its own words, the evaluations published since, graded by design, and a running verdict. Every entry follows the Unlearn shape: start from why the intuition feels right, ask the reader to guess, then show what the evidence found, graded honestly by study design.

# System test

- Loop: A daily beat searches open evaluation and trial registries for each case, adds new evaluations, re-grades the verdict when evidence piles up, and logs every change of verdict with its reason.
- Measure: share of cases whose evaluations were searched within 7 days, target 1.0; verdict changes logged with a reason, never silent
- Upkeep: new studies, data releases and evaluations arrive every week, sources rot and entries must be re-graded
- Run cost: 5 USD a month for the back end

# Hook

- Guess the verdict: did this famous policy deliver what it promised? Then the evaluations. The surprise, either way, is the hook.

# Public good

- Citizens, journalists and staff can hold policy to its own promises, so public money moves to what works; it empowers voters with evidence.

# Why people return

- Readers follow cases and see what changed since their last visit; a verdict that moved is the reason to come back.

# First outcomes

- Phase 1: casebook of 40 policies with stated aims and graded evaluations
- Phase 2: verdict history and follow on any case
- Phase 3: prediction market of the system's own: forecast the next evaluation, scored when it lands

# Constraints

- Budget per month: 5
- No accounts, cookies or trackers; reader taps are counted per entry with nothing that identifies the reader
- Every number on a page comes from a cited, graded source; nothing is typed without one
