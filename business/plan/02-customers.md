# Customers and how to apply Ultrastable

*Voice: the sales manager, with customer success on the "how to apply" part.*

## Who buys first

The best first customer looks like Rowan: someone with more projects than people, who already uses AI coding agents and is tired of being their manager.

| Segment | The buyer | Their pain | What Ultrastable does for them | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Solo founders and indie builders** running 3+ projects | The founder | Spends the day re-prompting agents, checking their work and deciding trivial things | Runs each project's backlog, checks the work, only asks about irreversible steps | **Beachhead** (fast sale, low price, loud voice) |
| **Agencies and dev shops** | Owner or head of delivery | Margins depend on how many client projects one lead can supervise | One lead oversees many client projects; every client gets the same org kit and audit trail | **Second** (higher price, repeatable) |
| **Small engineering teams** (5 to 50 engineers) | CTO, VP Engineering, head of platform | Agents produce code but nobody owns the backlog, review load explodes, maintenance crowds out roadmap | Managers above the agents; reserved time for improvement; independent review | **Third** (bigger contracts, longer sales) |
| **Research labs, nonprofits, open-source maintainers** | PI, program lead, maintainer | Little staff, lots of ongoing work | Same, with grant-friendly reporting | Opportunistic; good for case studies and grants |
| **Business functions beyond software** (marketing, ops, research) | Department head | Same management problem, different work | The roles are configuration, so the same structure runs any function | Later; proves "any organization" |

## Use cases to lead with

1. **"My agents need a manager."** Turn a pile of agent sessions into a managed backlog with owners and done checks.
2. **Many projects, one person.** A portfolio view that allocates attention across projects automatically.
3. **Maintenance without stagnation.** Keep fixing what breaks while a fixed share of time goes to making things better.
4. **Autonomy with an audit trail.** Every decision and result is logged, so a person can review any of it later.
5. **New project in a day.** The org kit starts a project with roles, gates and a backlog already in place.

## How to apply Ultrastable to an organization (the org kit)

*Customer success runs this; it is the same path Ultrastable itself is following in the [roadmap](../../docs/roadmap.md).*

1. **Map the org chart.** Write down the real roles (or the ones they wish they had). Each becomes a virtual manager or staff role in a config file.
2. **State the objectives.** Three to five outcomes the organization wants this quarter. Managers rank all work against them.
3. **Connect the work.** Repos, issue tracker, cloud account, docs. Read-only first.
4. **Set the gate.** Agree, in writing, which steps always need a human: spending money, signing, outside communication, deleting data without a backup. That list stays short.
5. **Start in observe mode.** Agents propose; people approve. Every result is checked independently and logged.
6. **Let autonomy rise with the record.** As agents prove themselves on a kind of work, they get more freedom on that kind of work. The customer can always override.
7. **Weekly review.** A four-line report per project: what happened, what was decided, what's next, what's open.
8. **Make it repeatable.** Save the setup as the organization's own kit for its next project.

Talking about step 6 in public: "it gets more independent as it proves itself"; the method is in the open repositories.

## Discovery call script

- "How many projects are you running, and how many people?"
- "How do you use AI coding agents today? Who manages them?"
- "What did you have to decide yourself last week that you wish you hadn't?"
- "What would you never let an agent do without asking?"
- "If something ran your backlog and only asked about the things on that list, what would that be worth per month?"

The sales manager logs each call's answers in `customers/discovery-log.md` with date and segment.
